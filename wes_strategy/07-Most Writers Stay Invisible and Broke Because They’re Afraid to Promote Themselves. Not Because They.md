# Most Writers Stay Invisible and Broke Because They’re Afraid to Promote Themselves. Not Because They Can’t Write.

### You have more control over your writing career than any previous generation of writers—but nobody is coming to promote your work for you.

This may sound harsh, but I think a lot of writers need to hear it.

Most writers don’t have a writing problem.

*They have a promotion problem.*

They’ll spend five hours writing a Post, publish it, share the link once, and immediately start working on the next one.

Then, when their Substack barely grows, they assume the writing wasn’t good enough.

***So they write more.***

They spend longer polishing every sentence. They publish more frequently. They keep sharing new work with the same small audience while hoping the right people will eventually find them.

But your writing may not be the problem.

***You may already be writing something worth reading. Not enough people are seeing it.***

## **No one is coming to promote your writing for you**

Traditionally, writers often had other people helping them build a career.

An agent helped them find opportunities. A publisher helped them reach readers. A marketing team helped promote and sell their books.

When you’re a solo writer online, nobody is automatically doing those things for you.

***You have to become your own promoter.***

That’s the part many writers don’t enjoy.

They don’t want to repeat themselves. They don’t want to sound annoying. They don’t want people to think they’re trying too hard.

So they publish a Post, share it once, and move on…

***But your readers don’t see everything you share.***

They miss Posts. They scroll past Notes. They save something for later and forget to return.

You see every time you promote your work, so it feels repetitive to you.

*To everyone else, it probably doesn’t.*

One Post can give you several different ways to promote the same idea:

- Share the strongest opinion from it.
- Tell the personal story behind it.
- Pull out one useful lesson.
- Turn the main idea into a question.
- Restack it later with a new perspective.

You don’t always need to write something new.

***You need to give your best ideas more opportunities to reach the right people.***

## **There has never been a better time to be a solo writer**

Having to promote yourself may feel uncomfortable, but it also comes with an enormous amount of freedom.

- You don’t need an agent to choose you.
- You don’t need a publisher to give you permission.
- You don’t need a book deal before you can earn money from your ideas.
- You can publish something today and immediately put it in front of readers.

You can use Notes to introduce your ideas to people who have never heard of you. You can build a direct relationship with your audience. You can create digital products that solve their problems and allow you to earn from what you know.

There’s an entire group of readers on Substack waiting to find writers they connect with.

*But they can’t find you if you’re afraid to promote yourself.*

And they can’t buy from you if you never tell them what you have to offer.

That doesn’t mean every Post needs to become a sales pitch.

It means being willing to clearly and consistently explain how your work can help someone.

If you’ve created something that genuinely solves a problem, promoting it isn’t selfish.

*Keeping it hidden doesn’t help anybody.*
