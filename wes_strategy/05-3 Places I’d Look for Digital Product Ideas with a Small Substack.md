# 3 Places I’d Look for Digital Product Ideas with a Small Substack

> Source: https://substack.com/home/post/p-218399782

I got an email from Mark today that made me realize I’d left something out.

In a Post last week, I suggested paying attention to the questions readers ask in your comments and replies.

When the same problem keeps coming up, that can be a good starting point for a digital product.

But Mark asked:

> “What if I don’t have a lot of comments or replies yet? How do I know what to create?”

*That’s a fair question.*

When you’re new to Substack, you might spend hours writing a Post and get almost no response. Then someone tells you to “listen to your audience,” and you wonder what you’re supposed to listen to.

*Maybe you want your writing to become another income stream…*

*Maybe you’d love to take on fewer clients or give yourself more options beyond your day job…*

But growing an audience feels slow. Now it sounds like you have to wait for a bigger one before you can even figure out what to sell.

**You can start researching your first idea before your own comments section gets busy.**

Here are three places I’d look.

### **1. Other writers’ readers are asking questions you can learn from**

Find three or four established Substack writers covering subjects similar to yours. Read their Posts, then spend some time in the comments.

- What do readers keep asking?
- Where are they confused?
- Are they requesting an example or saying they understand the advice but don’t know how to use it?

*Write down the questions that come up more than once.*

You’re looking for a problem you could help solve through your own experience. Those conversations can help you understand what people interested in your subject need—even before they find your Substack.

### **2. The bad book reviews may tell you more than the good ones**

Go to Amazon and find books about your subject.

Read the positive reviews to understand what readers found helpful. *Then read the critical ones.*

Sometimes someone explains exactly what they hoped the book would help them do—and what was missing.

- *“Good information, but I needed examples.”*
- *“I still don’t know where to start.”*
- *“I wanted something I could actually use.”*

A gap like that could suggest a short guide, a template, or a focused workshop.

For example, someone might enjoy a book about gardening but still need a simple planting plan for a small balcony. You could help with that specific step without writing another gardening book.

### **3. Reddit is full of people explaining where they’re stuck**

Find communities related to what you write about and read the conversations.

Look for repeated questions. *Pay attention to what people have tried, what frustrated them, and what they still need help doing.*

Notice the words they use, too. When you eventually describe your product, you want someone to recognize their problem in your offer.

You’re looking for a pattern you understand well enough to help with. One conversation might spark an idea; several people describing the same struggle give you more to work with.

---

### **You’re looking for one problem—not ten**

I learned this after my first digital product flopped.

I spent weeks making what I thought readers needed. When I finally asked what they were struggling with, their answers pointed me toward simpler, more useful ideas.

***Creating products around those problems has helped me make over $200K in the last two years as a writer, working on this part time.***

You don’t have to turn everything you know into your first product. A useful guide, template, or workshop can help someone take one step they’ve been struggling with.

Keep the idea focused enough that you could create a first version over a weekend. Then test your understanding through a Post, a Note, or a conversation with someone who has that problem before spending weeks building.

*Your job today is to find a starting point.*

Collect five questions from your research. Which one are you qualified to help answer? What could you make that would help someone put your answer into practice?
