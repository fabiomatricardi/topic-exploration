My first digital product was a PDF guide, around 25 pages, covering what I’d figured out about growing on Substack.

I put it together over a weekend, priced it under $50, and mentioned it in a post.

*Within 72 hours, 23 people had bought it.*

I’d earned money from my writing before. As a resume writer and career coach, I knew how to find clients and fill a calendar.

*But every dollar from that work came with a call to prepare for, a document to write, or an appointment to show up for.*

This was different. I had made something once, and people were buying it simply because they wanted the help inside. Nobody was waiting on me to get on a call.

That made me look at my whole Substack differently.

### **I Almost Talked Myself Out of Writing that First Guide**

Readers kept sending me versions of the same questions.

- *“How are you growing your Substack?”*
- *“What’s your writing strategy?”*
- *“Can you help me with mine?”*

I answered each one individually, sometimes spending fifteen or twenty minutes on a single reply, and then another reader would send me almost the same question the next day.

*I still hesitated to turn any of it into a product.*

I kept telling myself that someone with more experience was better qualified, that a guide like this should come from a bigger name than mine.

*But the people asking weren’t looking for the world’s leading expert.*

They just wanted help with something I had already figured out, and they came to me because I was a few steps ahead of where they were.

So instead of trying to teach everything about writing online, I organized the answers I kept typing out and turned them into a guide they could follow on their own.

### **Those 23 Buyers Showed Me Something Pretty Big**

A growing audience feels encouraging, but a follower count can’t tell you whether anyone values your help enough to pay for it. *Those 23 purchases could.*

The relationship also didn’t end at checkout.

Some buyers started showing up more in my comments, a few shared my writing, and others reached out asking what else I could help with.

*Their feedback showed me which parts of the guide were most useful and what I should build next.*

My Substack stopped being only a place where I published and became a place where I understood my readers’ problems and offered practical help with them.

### **You Don’t Need a Bigger Audience to Start. You can start this week.**

Since then, I’ve created other products around specific problems my readers face.

***Together they’ve brought in over $200,000 in the last two years, and I work on this part time.***

If you’re waiting for your list to get bigger before you create anything to sell, I’d gently push back on that.

I’m glad I didn’t wait. *Those first buyers taught me what readers valued and gave me something to build on.*
